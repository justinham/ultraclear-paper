**The frozen encoder is the wall that prevents collapse.** The adversary's job is to make `c` uninformative about env, but it can only do that by pushing the h_phi + FiLM modules (since the encoder is locked). The location-relevant signal lives in the encoder features, untouched, so the location head keeps learning throughout. In the no-freeze run, the adversary could push the encoder itself toward a constant, killing location as a side effect.

### What this tells us about Tier 2 FiLM (the parked item you asked to start with)

1. **The FiLM+adversary pipeline can work** — train RMSE 0.34m and val 2.52m at peak are real, not trivial-solution artifacts. So FiLM *can* disentangle without collapsing the encoder, given the right regularization.

2. **The frozen-encoder trick is doing a lot of heavy lifting** — without it, the same pipeline collapses. This is a structural concern for the joint tier1+2 trainer where the encoder is also being MAML-adapted. The progressive-unfreeze mechanism (7/15) might still be needed there.

3. **The best val RMSE (2.52 m at ep 40) is achieved when probe_env_acc is 0.955** — i.e. when the disentanglement is *failing*. This is the 7/10 K-shot trap in disguise: the best OOD generalization happens *before* disentanglement is fully effective. The "sweet spot" is probably the ramp window (ep 15-40), not the fully-converged state.

4. **The cond head and NLOS head are starving** — both have low weight (0.5 and 0.1 respectively) and both lose to the adversary. If you want either to work, the loss balance needs to change, or you need a different disentangle mechanism (e.g. conditional info bottleneck instead of pure adversary).

5. **Ep 500 is worse than ep 40 for val** — train continues to improve (0.34 → 0.55 m RMSE) but val degrades (2.52 → 3.89 m). This is **OOD overfitting**: the model keeps squeezing the train set while the disentanglement pressure slowly corrupts the val-relevant features. **Stop at ep 40 next time, not 500.**
