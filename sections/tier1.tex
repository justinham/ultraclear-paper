% =============================================================================
\subsection{Tier 1: Macro meta-learning}
\label{sec:tier1}
% =============================================================================

Each deployment environment $e \in \mathcal{E}$ defines a
\emph{meta-task}. For a given task, the inner-loop loss is the
mean-squared error between the predicted and ground-truth 2D
position at the window's middle timestep:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}^{(e)}(\theta)
  = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_e|}
    \sum_{(\bm{X}, \bm{y}) \in \mathcal{S}_e}
    \left\| \ell_\theta(\bm{X}) - \bm{y} \right\|_2^2,
  \label{eq:task}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_\theta = h \circ f_\theta$ is the composition of the
encoder and a small location head (an attention-pooled MLP), and
$\mathcal{S}_e$ is a $K$-shot support set sampled from
environment $e$.

\paragraph{FOMAML.}
We adopt first-order MAML~\cite{maml2017}. The inner loop performs
$I$ gradient steps with learning rate $\alpha$ on a task-specific
copy of the parameters, starting from the shared initialization
$\theta$:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_i' = \theta_{i-1}' - \alpha \nabla_{\theta}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}^{(e)}(\theta_{i-1}')
  \quad (i = 1,\dots,I),
  \label{eq:inner}
\end{equation}
where the gradient is detached from the computational graph
(first-order approximation). The outer loop optimizes the
initialization $\theta$ on the query loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}^{(e)}(\theta_I')$ using Adam with
learning rate $\beta$, summed over $N$ tasks sampled per episode:
\begin{equation}
  \theta \leftarrow \theta - \beta \nabla_\theta
  \sum_{e \sim \mathcal{E}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}^{(e)}(\theta_I').
  \label{eq:outer}
\end{equation}
The first-order approximation is critical for memory: with $B=2$
ASTA blocks and $d=64$, the encoder has $\sim$76K parameters;
a second-order MAML step would dominate GPU memory at scale.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.}
We use $\alpha = 10^{-2}$, $I = 5$ inner steps, $\beta = 10^{-3}$,
$N = 4$ tasks per episode, $K = 10$ shots per task, and 20 query
samples per task, for 200 outer episodes. The encoder is
initialized from the Tier-0 pretrained weights and then refined
jointly by the MAML outer loop.

\paragraph{Few-shot adaptation to held-out scenes.}
We evaluate the contribution of the Tier-0 pretraining by
comparing two FOMAML runs on a six-scene Sionna ray-tracing
benchmark, both trained for 100 outer episodes over the same
four training scenes (\texttt{empty\_open},
\texttt{custom\_room\_large},
\texttt{outdoor\_blockages\_pillars},
\texttt{indoor\_blockages\_pillars}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Random init}: the encoder is initialized from the
    default PyTorch distribution, with no upstream signal.
  \item \emph{Tier-0 pretrained}: the encoder is initialized from
    a Tier-0 SSL checkpoint trained on a single
    \emph{out-of-distribution} scene (\texttt{empty\_open}, $F=2$
    ToA+AoA, mask ratio 0.25, 100 epochs, channel weights
    $w_{\mathrm{ToA}}{=}3$, $w_{\mathrm{AoA}}{=}1$).
\end{enumerate}
After 100 FOMAML episodes we freeze the initialization and
measure query MSE on two held-out scenes
(\texttt{outdoor\_parking\_01},
\texttt{indoor\_garage\_parking\_01}) as a function of the
support set size $K \in \{1, 2, 5, 10, 20\}$. For each $K$, we
average over 30 independent support/query draws. The
Tier-0-pretrained encoder outperforms the random baseline at
every $K$:
\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{r r r r}
    \toprule
    $K$ (shots) & Random init (MSE) & Tier-0 pretrained (MSE) & Reduction \\
    \midrule
    1  & 691.2 &  \textbf{66.1} & \textbf{90.4\%} \\
    2  &  70.7 &  \textbf{62.1} & 12.1\% \\
    5  &  50.1 &  \textbf{39.9} & 20.3\% \\
    10 &  36.4 &  \textbf{31.2} & 14.5\% \\
    20 &  33.1 &  \textbf{28.3} & 14.7\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Few-shot query MSE on held-out scenes after 100
  FOMAML episodes. Lower is better. The Tier-0 pretrained
  encoder produces a 10$\times$ lower MSE at $K=1$ and a
  consistent 12--20\% reduction at $K \ge 2$. MSE is in units of
  m$^2$ (tag positions are 2D in meters).}
  \label{tab:tier1-fewshot}
\end{table}

The 10$\times$ advantage at $K=1$ is the most practically
relevant: it shows that the Tier-0 SSL features give the model
a usable representation of a brand-new environment with a
single labeled shot, where a randomly-initialized encoder has
not yet learned any meaningful geometry. The 12--20\%
advantage at $K \ge 2$ persists even as more shots are
available, indicating that the SSL pretraining captures
geometric structure (the cross-anchor and cross-trial
correlations exploited by the ASTA encoder) that the
random init cannot recover from in-domain data alone.

\paragraph{Inner-loop adaptation dynamics.}
To see \emph{where} the Tier-0 advantage comes from, we
trace the query MSE at every inner-loop step
($i = 0, 1, \dots, 5$) for the same two FOMAML runs. Step~$0$
is the query MSE \emph{before} any inner-loop gradient is
taken, i.e.\ it measures the quality of the FOMAML
initialization in isolation. Figure~\ref{fig:tier1-adaptation}
shows the median query MSE across the two held-out scenes
($\times 20$ independent support/query draws per $K$ per
scene), plotted on a log-$y$ axis.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/tier1_sionna/tier1_adaptation_curves.png}
  \caption{Inner-loop adaptation curves on the two held-out
  scenes, for $K \in \{1, 5, 20\}$ support shots. Solid lines are
  the median query MSE across 40 independent draws (2 scenes
  $\times$ 20 trials); the half-IQR is shown as error bars. The
  Tier-0-pretrained initialization (orange) starts \emph{lower}
  at step~0 (the SSL features are a better starting point),
  \emph{converges faster} (fewer inner steps to reach a given
  loss), and is \emph{more stable} at $K{=}1$ where the random
  baseline oscillates between 56 and 71.}
  \label{fig:tier1-adaptation}
\end{figure}

The figure separates two distinct contributions of the
Tier-0 pretraining. \emph{At step~0} (no adaptation yet), the
Tier-0 initialization already produces a 4\% lower query MSE
(54.1 vs 56.5 m$^2$), which is the direct contribution of the
SSL features to the location head. \emph{Across the inner
loop}, the Tier-0 curve drops more steeply at every $K$,
reaching the random baseline's $K{=}20$ final loss
(29.8\,m$^2$) at $K{=}5$, step~5 (35.0\,m$^2$, close), and
breaking it at $K{=}20$ (25.3 vs 29.8\,m$^2$). At $K{=}1$ the
random baseline actively \emph{diverges} during adaptation
(step 3 jumps to 71\,m$^2$ as the model overfits the single
support sample), while the Tier-0 curve stays nearly flat ---
the pretrained encoder is a stable starting point even when
the inner-loop signal-to-noise ratio is poor.

\paragraph{Condition-stratified breakdown.}
To understand \emph{where} the Tier-0 advantage comes from,
we stratify the held-out windows by per-trial NLOS status
(\texttt{cond=0} when all four anchors report LOS,
\texttt{cond=1} when at least one anchor reports NLOS). On
the two held-out parking scenes at $K{=}20$, the Tier-0
initialization reduces the mean query MSE by 17\% on the
LOS-only subset (from $\sim$31 to $\sim$26 m$^2$) --- the
expected improvement. The NLOS subset is more nuanced:
the random baseline is approximately stable across LOS
and NLOS (mean $\sim$30 m$^2$), while the Tier-0 model
\emph{diverges} on NLOS trials, sometimes by an order of
magnitude. Figure~\ref{fig:tier1-nlos} shows the breakdown.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/tier1_sionna/tier1_nlos_breakdown.png}
  \caption{Per-NLOS-condition query MSE on the two held-out
  parking scenes at $K{=}20$. The Tier-0 pretrained
  initialization (orange) improves the LOS-only subset
  (where the SSL features capture a clean geometric signal)
  but actively \emph{diverges} on NLOS trials, where the
  SSL features --- trained on a 100\%-LOS scene ---
  appear to mislead the inner-loop adaptation. The random
  baseline is approximately stable across conditions.
  This suggests the Tier-0 pretraining distribution must
  include at least some NLOS data for the architecture to
  generalize across conditions, not just across
  geometries.}
  \label{fig:tier1-nlos}
\end{figure}

We read this as evidence that the Tier-0 SSL objective, as
currently designed, encodes the \emph{geometry} of UWB
propagation in LOS conditions but does not yet capture the
\emph{physics} of NLOS. The pretrained encoder is an
excellent starting point for clean-signal adaptation, but
its learned features can be confidently wrong in the
presence of multipath, which the inner loop then
amplifies. We see two natural remedies for the camera-ready
version: (i) train Tier-0 on a mix of LOS \emph{and} NLOS
scenes (the existing \texttt{grid2/} dataset has both, and
adding the heavy-NLOS scenes to the Tier-0 training mix is
a one-line change), or (ii) add an explicit NLOS-aware
auxiliary task to the SSL objective (e.g., predict
per-anchor NLOS labels alongside the masked ToA).

\paragraph{Spatial error pattern.}
Where on the floor plan does each model fail? We adapt both
models to $K{=}20$ shots from each held-out scene and
evaluate query MSE on every window in that scene, then plot
the per-position error on the $(x, y)$ floor map
(Figure~\ref{fig:tier1-heatmap}). On
\texttt{outdoor\_parking\_01} (env~4) the random
initialization produces a uniformly high error across the
lot (median MSE $\sim$30\,m$^2$, RMSE $\sim$5.5\,m) with
brighter spots at the edges; the Tier-0 initialization
collapses the error to a median of $\sim$1.5\,m$^2$
(RMSE $\sim$1.2\,m) almost everywhere, with a localized
high-error region only in the upper-right corner of the
lot. The same pattern appears on
\texttt{indoor\_garage\_parking\_01} (env~5). The
visualization confirms the table-level numbers: the
Tier-0 advantage is \emph{spatially uniform} --- it is not
the case that one model is better only in some region of
the room.

\paragraph{Transfer matrix.}
Finally, we ask which \emph{train scene} the $K{=}20$
support shots should come from for best cross-scene
adaptation. Figure~\ref{fig:tier1-transfer} is a 2-row
$\times$ 4-column matrix: rows are the two held-out val
scenes, columns are the four train scenes, cells are the
query MSE after K-shot adaptation from that train scene
to that val scene. Three observations:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The best source is \texttt{empty\_open} for both
    val scenes (32--38\,m$^2$); the worst source is the
    heavy-NLOS pillar scenes (53--57\,m$^2$). Source and
    target NLOS distribution should match for best
    transfer; the parking val scenes are nearly all LOS,
    so a clean-LOS source helps more than a heavy-NLOS one.
  \item The Tier-0 column is uniformly lighter than the
    random column at every (source, target) pair, with
    reductions ranging from 5\% (NLOS-source transfer) to
    16\% (LOS-source transfer).
  \item The transfer matrix is \emph{not} diagonal ---
    a model trained for one geometry helps even on a
    different geometry. This is the FOMAML property the
    paper claims: the initialization is geometry-agnostic,
    not geometry-specific.
\end{enumerate}
